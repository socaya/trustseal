Trust & security

What TRUSTSEAL cryptographically proves, what it records as workflow evidence and what it deliberately does not claim. This page is the canonical statement of our assurance scope.

What we prove cryptographically

  • Platform-sealed PDFs. Each finalized envelope is sealed with a TRUSTSEAL-controlled PAdES-B-B signature backed by an AWS KMS-held key. An independent verifier can confirm the sealed bytes were signed by our platform certificate and have not been altered since.
  • RFC 3161 trusted timestamps. Every seal captures a signed Time-Stamp Authority response bound to the sealed document digest. The TSA response is retained inside the validation dossier.
  • OCSP + CRL revocation evidence. At seal time we capture OCSP responses (and CRLs where applicable) for the certificates involved. These responses are stored with the evidence dossier so a later check can see what was true at sealing.
  • Chained-hash audit log. Every significant action — envelope prepared, decision recorded, seal written, admin operation — appends a SecurityEvent whose hash is chained from the previous event. Signed anchors pin the chain tip so any retrospective rewrite produces a detectable break.
  • Ed25519 review receipts. Each recorded signer decision gets a canonical, Ed25519-signed receipt. Verifiers can replay the receipt against a trusted public key without calling TRUSTSEAL.

What we record as workflow evidence

  • Signer appearances. A drawn signature captured during review is stored as a visual representation of consent. It is not a cryptographic signature on the sealed PDF.
  • Supporting attachments. Files the signer uploads alongside their decision are hashed and the hashes are included in the signed review receipt, so the receipt attests to the exact bytes.
  • Organizational stamps. A visible stamp is a workflow record that an authorized custodian applied a specific design. It is not a cryptographic organizational seal on its own; the authoritative seal is the platform PAdES signature.
  • Operator activity. Who prepared, reissued, withdrew, archived, labelled or transferred an envelope — every operation lands in SecurityEvent and the per-envelope timeline.

What TRUSTSEAL does not claim

TRUSTSEAL does not represent the current build as legally certified, universally binding, tamper-proof or compliant with a specific jurisdiction. We do not emit "qualified electronic signature" status unless the operator has independently configured a QSCD integration. We do not derive signer identity from a drawn signature. We do not guarantee the truth of the document's underlying claims — verification can only establish properties of the recorded bytes and evidence.

How verification runs

  1. You upload the sealed PDF and the evidence digest supplied by the issuer.
  2. We hash the uploaded bytes server-side; the file is discarded immediately after hashing.
  3. The embedded platform signature is validated against the pinned signing certificate.
  4. The response tells you what was checked, what passed, what failed and what we could not check — never conflating the four.

Operating assurances

  • PostgreSQL row-level security enforces tenant isolation as defence in depth.
  • Pluggable private-object storage (filesystem or S3/MinIO) keeps retained artifacts off the generic static server.
  • Rate limiting is DB-backed so per-worker ceilings do not multiply across the cluster.
  • Idempotency keys + a transaction outbox ensure replayed webhook deliveries and API retries don't double-write.
  • Reusable OpenTelemetry + Prometheus instrumentation. Every HTTP response carries X-Request-Id and X-Trace-Id for support correlation.
  • No third-party CDN assets are required for the authenticated UI; brand and design tokens are self-hosted.

Reporting a problem

Correlation IDs appear on error pages and in every response header. Please include the X-Request-Id when contacting support. Security issues should be reported through the channel established with your TRUSTSEAL administrator.
